CARES Weekly Report, Nov 6

Goal of the project: run the ReXNet-150 audio classifier on the FINALE accelerator chip design, on the DE10-Pro FPGA board. This week the old 2022 demo ran on the board for the first time, the chip simulation became about 470 times faster, and the first 26 ReXNet operations (the stem and blocks f0 to f3) were tested one by one. Ten bugs were found and fixed; six problems remain.

What was done

  • Remote power-on for the lab PC. A small Ethernet board on the lab network now sends the "wake up" packet; the PC can be started from a cold power-off over the internet. Power-cycling the PC also resets the FPGA board.
  • First working FPGA run. After the card was reseated, the 2022 FPGA image loaded and the PC found the board on PCIe. The 2022 demo processed 500 images at 32.5 fps on the FPGA (25.5 fps including the PC side). Its output has never been compared with the original model, so this shows that the board runs, not that its results are correct.
  • Simulation 470× faster. 99.6 % of simulation time was the simulated memory chip in the test bench, which re-copied its whole 256 MB on every write. A one-line change fixed it; results are bit-identical.
  • Test flow for one operation at a time. New scripts build one test per operation from the real model data and run all 26 operations of stage 1 (stem to block f3) in under a minute.
  • First results and first fixes. 7 operations passed at once. Four debug investigations ran in parallel on the failing operations. Six firmware and script bugs were fixed. Three bugs in the multiply unit of the chip design were proven and fixed.
  • Sigmoid unit fixed. A bug in its "too large" check was proven and fixed; four sigmoid operations now pass.
  • Second debug round on standalone depthwise convolution and on wide 1×1 convolution. No root cause found; both parked by decision.
  • Found that 82 chip-design files are identical to the 2022 design that works on the board. Every confirmed bug so far is in parts the previous student added or changed.
  • First FPGA rebuild. The 2022 FPGA project was rebuilt with only the October memory-transfer fix (Quartus, 6 h 14 min, finished Nov 5). Timing misses by up to 0.26 ns on the memory-controller clocks, the same as an unpatched rebuild in March. Not loaded on the board yet. A small PC program that runs the stem convolution on the board and compares it with the simulation is ready.
  • Checked the sigmoid unit: it is not on the FPGA yet. The board runs the 2022 image, which has no sigmoid or multiply unit. The new sigmoid unit (a 1024-entry lookup table for 32 channels at once) needs an FPGA rebuild.

The 26 operations today

Why stage 1 stops at block f3

The stem and blocks f0 to f3 already contain every kind of operation in the network and its largest images (112×112, 56×56). Later blocks only repeat these kinds at smaller sizes, and their firmware wraps each block in one call that must be split before testing.

An operation is one step the chip runs on its own. One ReXNet layer can need several: the stem is a convolution (00) followed by a SiLU activation, which the model file stores as a sigmoid (01) and a multiply (02). An operation passes when every output byte is within ±1 of the correct answer, which is the original model run on a PC (±1 is normal rounding). Each operation is tested alone, starting from the correct input; errors adding up across operations are not tested yet.
